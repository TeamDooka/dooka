Privacy policy

Last updated: 11 November 2026

Translation. This English version is provided for convenience. If it differs from the French version, the French version prevails.

Dooka is an app for friends, not an advertising product. This page describes exactly what is collected, why, and how to erase all of it. It’s written to be read.

In short. No advertising, no trackers, no third-party analytics tools, no selling of data. Your data is used to make the app work, and nothing else. You can delete your account from the app at any time: your personal data goes with it, with one exception, described below.

1. Who is responsible

Dooka is published by Pierre Laurent, a private individual, in a non-professional capacity. His contact details are given in the legal notice.

Contact for any question or request about your data: contact@dooka.fr

What stays on your phone

Some information is only saved on your device and never reaches us: the theme you chose (light, dark or automatic), the last map position, the current session, the intro screens you’ve already seen and the latest “What’s new” version you’ve read. It disappears when you uninstall the app.

What isn’t collected: no bank details, no advertising ID, no browsing history, no data from other apps. Dooka has no access to your PMU or FDJ accounts — the amounts you log are the ones you enter yourself.

3. Who it’s shared with

Dooka doesn’t sell or rent out any data. It only passes through the technical providers the app needs to work:

  • Supabase — hosting of the database and photos, on servers located in Paris, in the European Union
  • Google — signing in to your account, maps and place search
  • Apple — signing in to your Apple account, on iPhone
  • Expo — delivering notifications
  • Resend — sending emails (invitations, friend requests, updates on your suggestions, account deletion confirmation)
  • GitHub — hosting this website, the installation file and suggestion tracking. Each time it starts, the Android app asks GitHub whether a new version is available: as with any web page, GitHub then receives the phone’s IP address, but no data from your account
  • Anthropic (United States) — automatic sorting of the suggestions you send from the app (their title and description), before the team takes them up
  • Google Play and Apple TestFlight — distributing the app to testers

Transfers outside the European Union

Your data is hosted in France, by Supabase. But Google, Apple, Expo, Resend, GitHub and Anthropic are based in the United States: what they process for Dooka (sign-in, place search, notifications, emails, update checks, sorting suggestions) may therefore leave the European Union. These transfers rely on the safeguards provided for by the GDPR that these providers offer, such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses.

What other users see

Your name and profile photo are visible to the app’s other users, who can find you by your name.

The details of a session are only visible to its participants. Your friends and the members of your crews see your summary (tickets played, net, biggest win) and know when you’re in a session. Nobody else has access to your history.

A session’s creator chooses its players: they can add you to it and log tickets in your name.

One exception, visible to everyone: a bar’s biggest win. Your biggest win in a bar may appear, with your name and the amount, on that bar’s page. A win of €100 or more only appears there if you’ve attached the photo of the ticket. Your winnings also count in the leaderboard of the crews you belong to.

Finally, your tickets feed anonymous counters, visible to everyone: current activity in a bar, today’s plays, most-played tickets.

Photos are stored at an unlisted address: anyone who has the link can view them.

4. How long

Your data is kept as long as your account exists. It is erased when you delete your account, with no retention period, except for tickets played in sessions, which are anonymised (see below).

5. Deleting your account

In the app: tap your photo or your name at the top of the screen to open My profile, then, right at the bottom, Delete my account. Two confirmations, then the deletion is immediate and permanent. All that remains is an anonymous counter: the number of accounts deleted each day, and how long they had existed (less than a day, a week, a month, or longer), with nothing that could tell which accounts they were.

If you no longer have the app installed, the procedure is described on the Delete my account page.

One exception, in the interest of the other players. Your tickets played in sessions stay on record, under “Deleted player”, with their proof photos. Since version 3.10, this applies to all your tickets, even ones played alone: every ticket goes through a session. Otherwise, the totals for a night out and bars’ biggest wins would change retroactively for the other players. This record no longer contains your name, your email or your profile photo. The crews you created also stay, for their other members.

6. Your rights

The GDPR gives you the right to access, rectify, erase and port your data, and to restrict and object to its processing. Rectifying and erasing are done directly in the app; for everything else, write to contact@dooka.fr. You’ll get a reply within one month.

If you’re not satisfied with the reply, you can lodge a complaint with the CNIL (Commission nationale de l’informatique et des libertés, the French data protection authority).

7. Minimum age

Dooka deals with gambling and games of chance: its use is restricted to adults. The app doesn’t let you play, bet or receive money — it’s a tracking logbook and a tool for friends, with no link to PMU or to Française des Jeux.

From version 3.10, the app asks you once, after you sign in, to confirm that you’re 18 or over. Only the date of this confirmation is kept, with your account, and it disappears along with it. If you answer that you’re under 18, the app reminds you that it’s for over-18s only and offers to sign you out.

Gambling comes with risks: debt, isolation, addiction. Information and help at joueurs-info-service.fr (the French gambling helpline; 09 74 75 13 13, standard call rate). See also our responsible gambling page.

8. Changes

Any change to this policy will be published on this page, with an updated date. A significant change will be flagged in the app.
