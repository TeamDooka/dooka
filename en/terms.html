Translation. This English version is provided for convenience. If it differs from the French version, the French version prevails.

Dooka is a logbook for friends. These terms say what the app does, what it expects from you and what you can expect from it. Like the privacy policy, they’re written to be read.

In short. Dooka is free and for over-18s only. You don’t gamble on it: you log what you’ve played elsewhere. What you post stays yours, but it must respect others. Any content can be reported, and anything that breaks these rules is removed. You can leave at any time by deleting your account.

1. Who provides Dooka

Dooka is published by Pierre Laurent, a private individual acting in a non-professional capacity, whose contact details are given in the legal notice. In this text, “Dooka” and “we” mean the publisher.

These terms apply to the Dooka mobile app and to the dooka.fr website. By creating an account, you accept them. If you don’t accept them, don’t use the app.

2. What Dooka is, and what it isn’t

Dooka is for finding the PMU bars around you, keeping a logbook of what you play (scratch cards, nights at the PMU), opening a session with others and comparing your results with friends, within a crew.

Dooka is not a gambling operator. The app doesn’t let you play, bet, or deposit or receive money. It doesn’t award any prize or any reward. It is independent, with no link to PMU or to Française des Jeux (FDJ, the French national lottery operator).

The stakes and winnings shown are the ones users enter themselves: Dooka doesn’t check them. The leaderboards are a game between friends and have no official value.

Information given for guidance only

Bar listings come from Google and from users’ contributions: an address, opening hours or a phone number may be inaccurate or out of date. A ticket’s price, its odds of winning and its prize table are taken from public information: only the game operator’s information is authoritative.

3. Your account

  • You must be 18 or over. The app asks you to confirm this. An account whose holder turns out to be a minor is closed.
  • Your account is personal: one account per person, in your own name or under a username that doesn’t imitate anyone.
  • You’re responsible for what is done from your account. If you think someone else has access to it, write to contact@dooka.fr.
  • Dooka is free. Only your phone and your internet connection are at your own expense.

4. Gambling is still a risk

Dooka doesn’t encourage you to gamble, and will never help you to. The app gives you ways to stay in control: a weekly stake limit, a break, a time reminder. You set them in your profile, and they’re described on the responsible gambling page.

Gambling comes with risks: debt, isolation, addiction. Information and help at joueurs-info-service.fr (the French gambling helpline) or on 09 74 75 13 13 (standard call rate).

5. What you post

Your username, your profile photo, the name and image of your crews, your tickets and their photos, your sessions, your suggestions and what you add about a bar are your content. The privacy policy sets out who can see what.

This content stays yours. You only allow Dooka, free of charge and non-exclusively, to host it and show it to other users, solely to make the app work, and for as long as your account exists. Once your account is deleted, only the tickets played in sessions remain, without your name, as explained on the Delete my account page.

You guarantee that you have the right to post what you post. In particular, don’t post a photo of a recognisable person without their consent.

6. House rules

Dooka is made for crews of friends. You must not post or do any of the following on it:

  • illegal, hateful, discriminatory, violent, sexual or defamatory content;
  • harassment, insults and threats, including through the name of a crew or a session;
  • pretending to be someone else, Dooka, PMU or Française des Jeux;
  • someone else’s personal data (phone number, address, photo) without their consent;
  • adding someone to a session against their will, or assigning them tickets they didn’t play;
  • fake winnings, fake proof or false information about a bar;
  • advertising, soliciting and spam;
  • using the app to organise bets, loans or exchanges of money between users, or to resell tickets;
  • letting a minor use the app, or encouraging a minor to gamble;
  • getting around the app’s protections, extracting its data by automated means or disrupting how it works.

7. Reporting and blocking

A profile, a crew, a bar’s page, a ticket photo or a suggestion can be reported from the app, by choosing a reason. You can also write to contact@dooka.fr, saying which content you mean, where it is and why it’s a problem. You don’t need an account to do this.

You can also block a user from their profile, and change your mind at any time.

What we do with a report

Every report is read by a person on the team: no decision is made by a program. We look into it as soon as we can, normally within 24 hours.

Depending on how serious it is, we may remove the content, send a warning, suspend the account or close it. Closing an account is reserved for serious or repeated breaches. Clearly illegal content may be removed without waiting for a report, and passed on to the authorities where the law requires it.

The person concerned is told by email what action was taken and why. They can challenge it by replying to that message within six months: their request is then reviewed again by a person. Whoever made the report is also told what came of it, if they left a way to reach them.

Repeated and clearly unfounded reports may no longer be dealt with.

8. Your suggestions

The ideas and bugs you send from the app are visible to other users, who can vote for them. Before the team reads them, their title and description are sorted automatically by an artificial intelligence tool provided by Anthropic: don’t put any personal information in them.

We may use a suggestion freely, in whole or in part, without anything in return. We aren’t obliged to act on it.
