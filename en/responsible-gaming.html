Gambling should stay a game.

Dooka is a logbook, not a casino. We don’t take bets, we don’t pay out winnings, and the app has nothing to gain from you playing more. Here’s what it does and doesn’t do, and where to find help if gambling is taking up too much room.

What Dooka doesn’t do

These are deliberate choices, and the app sticks to them.

  • No jackpot in the spotlight. A ticket shows the share of tickets that pay back more than the stake, and its page lists every prize without putting any of them up front. And never a “what you could have won”: your summaries only count what was actually played and won.
  • No streaks to keep up. No “log in 7 days in a row”, no rewards for coming back often, no congratulations on a run of winning nights.
  • No countdowns, no deadlines. Nothing expires, nothing’s urgent.
  • No ads, no partnership with any gambling operator. Dooka is free and independent — it has no interest in you playing more.
  • No losers’ leaderboard. Comparisons between friends are based on real results, and they don’t set you a target: no podium, no crown, no gap to close. A crew’s leaderboard is just a record.

What was actually won

Dooka shows what was won, never what could be won. So some things do put real results on show: worth knowing before you start.

  • Crew leaderboards. A session ranks its players by net result, and every crew has its leaderboard.
  • Each bar’s best win. The biggest win logged in a bar shows on its page, with the player’s name and the amount, with no crown or trophy. A win of €100 or more only appears there if the ticket photo was attached.
  • Your friends’ big wins, if you ask for them. A notification when a friend wins €50 or ten times the price of their ticket. It’s off by default: you turn it on in the app’s notification settings, and it goes quiet during a break. Your own wins only trigger it for friends who’ve turned it on.
  • The Advent calendar, in December. From 1 to 24 December, one ticket of the day, from €1 to €5, the same for the whole community, with a shared counter: how many people scratched it, how many won, the biggest win, with no names. Nothing you have to do: you can follow it without buying anything, there are no days to catch up on, and its morning notification is off by default. It disappears during a break and stops offering a ticket once you’ve reached your budget for the week.
  • Each bar’s counters. Current activity, today’s plays, most-played tickets: anonymous numbers, across all players.

What the app gives you

Three tools, in your profile, under “My limits”.

  • A weekly budget. You set an amount, over a rolling seven days; the app never suggests one. It shows you where you stand against it, without ever stopping you — it’s a mirror, not a barrier.
  • A time reminder. A notification every 30, 45 or 60 minutes during a session, whichever you choose. Time flies at the bar.
  • A break. Take a break for 7, 30 or 90 days: the app hides “I scratched” and play logging, and mutes the time reminder, your friends’ big wins, the Advent calendar notification and friend request notifications. A banner reminds you when it ends; the plays you’ve already logged stay, and you can end the break any time.

And at any time, you can delete your account: your profile, your friends and your stats are erased for good. Only your tickets stay, without your name, so the totals of shared nights don’t change for everyone else.

Warning signs

Gambling becomes a problem long before you admit it to yourself. A few signs:

  • regularly playing for longer or bigger than you meant to;
  • playing again to “win it back” after a loss;
  • borrowing, or dipping into money meant for something else;
  • hiding how much you play from the people close to you;
  • thinking about it a lot, outside the times you play;
  • feeling irritable or empty when you can’t play.

Any doubt is reason enough to talk about it. The Joueurs Info Service self-assessment test takes a few minutes and it’s anonymous (in French).

Need to talk?

Joueurs Info Service (the French national gambling helpline) — anonymous, free, 7 days a week from 08:00 to 02:00.

09 74 75 13 13

Standard call rate. Also by chat and email at joueurs-info-service.fr, which also offers support for family and friends.

To ban yourself from gambling, you apply to the Autorité nationale des jeux (ANJ, the French gambling regulator): anj.fr.

What Dooka isn’t

Dooka doesn’t let you play, bet or receive money. It’s a tracking logbook and a tool for friends. The tickets and races it talks about are played elsewhere, at a retailer.
